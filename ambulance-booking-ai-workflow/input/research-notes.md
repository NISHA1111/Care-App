**sample research input for workflow demonstration**
All scenarios, statements and observations below are authored simulations. No participants were interviewed; no services were evaluated. IDs are traceability references, not empirical evidence. No age, income, diagnosis or demographic facts are supplied.

| ID | Simulated context | Sample observation | Relevant question |
|---|---|---|---|
| S01 | Family member arranges urgent transport | The requester wants one clear action and a visible call fallback rather than several choices on entry. | Q1, Q5 |

## Illustrative comparative input
These archetypes are invented patterns, not named competitors or current market findings.
C01: call-first directory — direct contact, but availability/type/estimates may be outside the interface.
C02: booking marketplace — compares service cards, but many choices may add decision effort.
C03: request-and-status interface — separates request received, provider assigned and arrival states; status must reflect actual system knowledge.

## Contradictions and limits
S01 favors speed while S07 favors explanation. Urgent and planned journeys may need different emphasis; no clinical urgency assessment is supported. S03 raises a risk in self-selection of type, not evidence for automated triage. S08 is a single illustrative scenario and cannot establish language demand.

## Evidence policy
Within this simulated dataset, strong means repeated in at least two scenarios; medium means one explicit scenario; weak means an inference. Every empirical claim remains unvalidated regardless of within-sample strength. No market statistics, real prices, ETAs, phone numbers or clinical advice are provided.

## Next research
